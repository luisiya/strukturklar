Thinking models

Risk – Resources – Goal

A model for examining business decisions in the context of objectives, available resources and possible risks.

01Initial problem

The model helps to avoid looking at decisions in isolation. A measure can achieve a goal while at the same time creating new risks or a high demand for resources.

02Guiding question

What goal should be achieved, what resources are available and what risks arise?

03Model

  1. Goal ↔ Resources ↔ Risks→
  2. Options→
  3. Impact

04Elements

  1. 1

    Goal

    • What should be achieved?
    • What impact is expected?
    • How is success measured?
  2. 2

    Resources

    • Staff
    • Time
    • Budget
    • Information
    • Technical options
    • Existing competencies
  3. 3

    Risks

    • Errors
    • Delays
    • Dependencies
    • Lack of capacity
    • Interface problems
    • Quality risks
    • Financial effects

05Example

An organisation wants to make a process faster.

  • Option A: more staff
  • Option B: restructure the process
  • Option C: partial automation
  • Option D: redistribute tasks

Each option has a different resource requirement, different risks and a different expected impact.

06Possible impact

Decisions become easier to compare.

07Limitations

The model provides a structured economic basis for decisions. It does not guarantee that a particular impact will actually occur.
